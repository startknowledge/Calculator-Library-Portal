How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County Public Library’s Tutoring & Learning Center is a cutting-edge platform designed to provide personalized academic support at no cost. Here’s how it functions:

1. Eligibility & Accessibility

  • Open to all Wake County residents, including students from public, private, and homeschool programs.
  • No income restrictions—unlike traditional tutoring services, this is 100% free.
  • Accessible via desktop, tablet, or smartphone through the library’s website.

2. Subject Coverage & Expert Tutors

The program offers tutoring in over 30 subjects, including:

  • Math (Algebra, Calculus, Statistics)
  • Science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)
  • English & Writing (Grammar, Essay Editing, SAT/ACT Prep)
  • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
  • College Prep (AP Courses, SAT/ACT, IB Support)

Tutors are certified educators, college students, and subject-matter experts with background checks for safety.

3. Scheduling & Availability

  • Live, one-on-one sessions (via video chat) 7 days a week.
  • Drop-in sessions for quick questions (no appointment needed).
  • Scheduled appointments for in-depth study sessions (up to 60 minutes per session).

4. Additional Resources

Beyond live tutoring, the platform includes:

  • Interactive practice quizzes
  • Video lessons & study guides
  • Access to e-books and research databases

5. How to Get Started

  1. Visit the WCPL Tutoring Portal (www.wakegov.com/library).
  2. Create a free account (using a Wake County library card).
  3. Browse available tutors and book a session.
  4. Join via Zoom or a web browser—no special software required.

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

While the service is easy to access, maximizing its benefits requires strategic planning. Here’s how to get the most out of your tutoring sessions:

1. Set Clear Goals Before Each Session

Problem: Many students walk into tutoring sessions without a specific plan, leading to wasted time. Solution:

  • Before booking, ask yourself:
    • What exact topic do I need help with?
    • What grade level is this material?
    • Do I need help with homework, test prep, or conceptual understanding?
  • Example: Instead of saying, "I need help with math," specify: "I’m struggling with solving quadratic equations for my Algebra II final."

2. Prepare Questions & Materials in Advance

Problem: Tutors can’t read minds—if you don’t prep your questions, the session may feel disjointed. Solution:

  • List 3-5 key questions before logging in.
  • Have your textbook, notes, or assignment ready for reference.
  • Example: If working on a chemistry problem, write down:
    • "Why does this reaction require a catalyst?"
    • "How do I balance this equation?"

3. Use Drop-In Sessions for Quick Doubts

Problem: Some students overbook scheduled sessions for minor questions, leaving no time for deeper learning. Solution:

  • For simple questions (e.g., "What’s the derivative of x²?"), use drop-in hours.
  • Save scheduled sessions for complex topics (e.g., "I don’t understand quantum mechanics concepts").

4. Record Sessions for Future Review

Problem: After a session, students often forget key takeaways because they didn’t document them. Solution:

  • Enable recording (if allowed) to review later.
  • Take notes during the session and summarize key points afterward.
  • Example: If your tutor explains how to solve integrals, write:
    • *"Method: Integration by parts (LIATE rule)"
    • "Formula: ∫u dv = uv - ∫v du"

5. Leverage Additional Resources

Problem: Some students only use live tutoring and miss out on free study materials. Solution:

  • Explore the library’s digital library for:
    • Khan Academy videos (aligned with Wake County standards)
    • Practice tests (SAT, ACT, AP exams)
    • Research papers (for high school/college assignments)
  • Example: If prepping for the SAT, use:
    • Official SAT practice tests (from the College Board)
    • Tutor-guided walkthroughs of tricky questions

6. Join Study Groups for Collaborative Learning

Problem: Tutoring is one-on-one, but group learning can reinforce concepts. Solution:

  • Ask your tutor about study groups (some libraries host virtual ones).
  • Form a small group with classmates to practice together.
  • Example: A physics study group could:
    • Work on Newton’s laws problems together
    • Quiz each other on electromagnetism concepts

7. Follow Up with Consistent Practice

Problem: Students get help in a session but don’t apply it, leading to no long-term improvement. Solution:

  • After each session, dedicate 30 minutes to practice what you learned.
  • Use the library’s quizzes to test your understanding.
  • Example: If your tutor helps with geometry proofs, spend time:
    • Drawing diagrams of theorems
    • Writing step-by-step proofs on your own

8. Provide Feedback to Improve the Service

Problem: Libraries don’t always know what students need until they share feedback. Solution:

  • After each session, rate your tutor and suggest improvements.
  • Contact WCPL directly with requests (e.g., "We need more AP Biology tutors").
  • Example Feedback:
    • "The SAT tutor was great, but could we get more sessions on essay writing?"
    • "I’d love a tutor who specializes in AP Computer Science."

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Free Tutoring Services

While Wake County’s program is exceptional, some students unintentionally sabotage their progress by making these critical errors:

1. Not Booking Sessions Early

Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to schedule a tutor. Why It Fails:

  • Popular tutors (e.g., AP Calculus, SAT Prep) book up fast.
  • Drop-in slots may be limited during peak hours (evenings/weekends). Solution:
  • Check availability weekly and book in advance.
  • Set calendar reminders for your sessions.

2. Treating Tutoring Like a Crutch (Not a Tool for Learning)

Mistake: Relying on the tutor to do the work instead of understanding concepts. Why It Fails:

  • Tutors explain, but they don’t memorize for you.
  • Passive learning leads to short-term fixes, not long-term success. Solution:
  • Ask "why" and "how" behind every step.
  • Practice independently after each session.

3. Ignoring the Library’s Additional Resources

Mistake: Only using live tutoring and missing out on free study materials. Why It Fails:

  • The library offers quizzes, videos, and e-books that reinforce learning.
  • Not using them means leaving free help on the table. Solution:
  • Explore the "Tutoring & Learning" section of the WCPL website.
  • Bookmark useful resources (e.g., Khan Academy links).

4. Not Following Up with Practice

Mistake: Getting help in a session but not applying it in homework or quizzes. Why It Fails:

  • Knowledge sticks when you use it.
  • Without practice, you’ll forget what the tutor taught. Solution:
  • Dedicate 30-60 minutes weekly to reinforce concepts.
  • Use the library’s practice quizzes to test yourself.

5. Being Too Shy to Ask Questions

Mistake: Holding back embarrassing or "stupid" questions out of pride. Why It Fails:

  • No question is too basic—every expert was once a beginner.
  • Unanswered doubts become long-term knowledge gaps. Solution:
  • Remember: Tutors are there to help, not judge.
  • Example Questions to Ask:
    • "I don’t understand this concept—can you explain it differently?"
    • "What’s a common mistake students make on this topic?"

6. Not Using the 24/7 Chat Feature

Mistake: Only using live video sessions and missing quick chat support. Why It Fails:

  • Chat is great for:
    • Quick homework checks
    • Defining terms (e.g., "What’s the difference between mitosis and meiosis?")
    • Last-minute exam prep Solution:
  • Check the "Chat with a Tutor" option for instant help.

7. Not Updating Your Profile

Mistake: Keeping the same profile with outdated goals (e.g., still needing help with 8th-grade math when you’re now in 12th grade). Why It Fails:

  • Tutors match students based on their profile.
  • If your needs change, they won’t get the right help. Solution:
  • Update your profile every semester to reflect your current coursework.
