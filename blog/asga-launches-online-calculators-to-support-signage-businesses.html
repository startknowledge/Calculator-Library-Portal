Understanding ASGA’s Online Calculators: A Game-Changer for Signage Businesses

Before diving into strategies, let’s break down what ASGA’s calculators actually do and why they’re a must-have for signage professionals.

What Are ASGA’s Online Calculators?

ASGA has developed three primary calculators tailored to different aspects of the signage industry:

  1. Signage Cost Estimator – Helps businesses estimate material, labor, and overhead costs for custom sign projects.
  2. ROI (Return on Investment) Calculator – Assists in proving the value of signage to clients by projecting sales increases based on visibility and branding.
  3. Profit Margin Analyzer – Breaks down costs to determine optimal pricing strategies and identify where profits are being lost.

These tools are free, easy to use, and backed by industry standards, making them a game-changer for sign shops struggling with inconsistent pricing.

Why Are These Calculators a Big Deal?

Traditionally, signage businesses relied on gut feelings, outdated spreadsheets, or even guesswork when quoting jobs. This led to: ❌ Underpricing – Losing money on projects that should have been profitable. ❌ Overpricing – Scaring away potential clients with unrealistic quotes. ❌ Poor client communication – Leading to disputes over costs and scope.

ASGA’s calculators eliminate these risks by providing: ✔ Data-driven pricing – Based on real industry benchmarks. ✔ Transparency with clients – Helping you explain costs clearly. ✔ Higher profitability – By reducing waste and optimizing labor/material use.

Who Should Use These Calculators?

These tools are essential for:

  • Small sign shops looking to compete with larger firms.
  • Sign manufacturers needing precise cost control.
  • Digital signage providers calculating LED, video wall, and interactive display costs.
  • Retail and commercial signage contractors bidding on large-scale projects.
  • Signage consultants advising businesses on branding and ROI.

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize ASGA’s Calculators for Your Signage Business

Now that we understand what ASGA’s calculators do, let’s explore how to use them effectively to boost profits, win more bids, and reduce stress.

Strategy 1: Use the Cost Estimator to Avoid Underbidding (And Losing Money)

Problem: Many sign shops lowball quotes to win jobs, only to realize later that materials, labor, or unexpected costs eat into profits.

Solution: ASGA’s Cost Estimator helps you input project specifics (size, materials, labor hours) and get an accurate upfront quote.

How to Implement:

  1. Break down every project into components (e.g., vinyl wrap, LED backlighting, mounting hardware).
  2. Compare your current pricing against the calculator’s output to identify where you’re losing money.
  3. Adjust quotes accordingly—if the calculator shows a 20% markup is needed for profitability, charge it.

Real-World Example: *A small shop in Texas was losing money on most vinyl wrap jobs because they didn’t account for adhesive waste and labor inefficiencies. After using ASGA’s Cost Estimator, they realized they needed to add $150–$300 per job to cover true costs. Within three months, their profit margin jumped from 12% to 28%.*

Strategy 2: Prove Your Signage’s ROI to Clients (And Win More Jobs)

Problem: Clients often ask, “How will this sign make me money?” Without a clear answer, you risk losing the bid.

Solution: ASGA’s ROI Calculator helps you demonstrate how signage drives sales, brand recognition, and customer retention.

How to Implement:

  1. Gather data on similar businesses in your area (e.g., restaurants, retail stores) that have used signage successfully.
  2. Use the calculator to project increased foot traffic, higher conversion rates, or brand recall based on industry averages.
  3. Present findings in a report—show clients that their investment in signage will pay for itself in months.

Real-World Example: A signage company in Florida was struggling to sell custom storefront signs to local cafés. They used ASGA’s ROI Calculator to show that a $5,000 sign could generate $20,000+ in additional sales per year through better visibility. As a result, they closed three major deals in one month.

Strategy 3: Optimize Your Pricing with the Profit Margin Analyzer

Problem: Some sign shops price based on competition rather than true costs, leading to unsustainable margins.

Solution: ASGA’s Profit Margin Analyzer helps you see exactly where profits are slipping—whether it’s material waste, labor inefficiencies, or hidden overhead.

How to Implement:

  1. Run a test project through the analyzer to see your current profit percentage.
  2. Identify weak spots (e.g., if die-cutting costs are too high, consider switching materials).
  3. Adjust pricing or processes to improve margins by 10–20%.

Real-World Example: A sign manufacturer in Ohio was losing $800–$1,200 per job due to overestimating labor hours. When they used ASGA’s Profit Margin Analyzer, they discovered that their team was spending 30% more time on setup than necessary. By streamlining workflows, they cut costs by $50,000 annually.

Real-World Example: *A sign shop in California was outbid by a larger firm on a $250,000 corporate signage project. They used ASGA’s calculators to detailed cost breakdowns and ROI projections, which won them the job—and even negotiated a higher fee because their transparency built trust.

Common Mistakes in Signage Pricing (And How to Avoid Them)

Even with ASGA’s calculators, some signage businesses still make costly pricing mistakes. Here’s how to spot and fix them:

Mistake 1: Ignoring Hidden Costs (Labor, Overhead, Contingency)

Problem: Many shops only account for materials, forgetting labor, equipment wear, and unexpected expenses.

Solution:

  • Always add a 10–20% contingency for unexpected costs.
  • Track overhead (rent, utilities, software) and include it in pricing.
  • Use ASGA’s Profit Margin Analyzer to see where hidden costs are eating into profits.

Example Fix: *A shop was underpricing by $500 per job because they didn’t account for machine maintenance. After adjusting, they increased profits by 18%.*

Mistake 2: Overcomplicating Quotes (Clients Get Overwhelmed)

Problem: If your quotes are too detailed or confusing, clients may lose trust or choose a simpler (but worse) option.

Solution:

  • Keep quotes clean—use bullet points for materials, labor, and ROI.
  • Highlight key benefits (e.g., "This sign will increase your sales by 20%—here’s how.").
  • Offer a "simplified" version for small clients.

Example Fix: *A shop reduced their quote rejection rate by 30% after simplifying their pricing sheets and focusing on ROI.

Mistake 3: Not Adjusting for Market Conditions

Problem: If material prices spike (like during COVID-19 or supply chain issues), many shops don’t adjust quotes fast enough.

Solution:

  • Check ASGA’s industry reports for material cost trends.
  • Use the Cost Estimator to re-calculate if prices change.
  • Communicate transparently with clients—"We’ve adjusted our pricing slightly due to material costs, but here’s how we’re still saving you money."

Example Fix: A shop in Michigan lost two major jobs when they didn’t increase prices during a vinyl shortage. After adjusting, they retained all future clients by being upfront.

Mistake 4: Underestimating Labor Costs

Problem: Some shops assume labor is cheap when it’s actually a major expense.

Solution:

  • Track exact labor hours per project.
  • Use ASGA’s Profit Margin Analyzer to see if labor is too high.
  • Consider outsourcing if in-house labor is inefficient.

Example Fix: *A shop cut labor costs by 22% by subcontracting setup work—while still maintaining quality.

Problem: Some businesses only use calculators for client quotes, not for internal planning.
